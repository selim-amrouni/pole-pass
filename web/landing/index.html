What public street photos can say about a town's utility poles

Mapillary holds millions of street-level photos with utility poles already outlined in them. Pole Pass takes every pole detection in a service territory, has a vision model look at the photos, and lays the result out as a map you can check pole by pole: type, visible equipment, attachments, and possible condition issues, each linked to its source photo.

Experimental. The model's assessments have not been independently verified, and a photo cannot establish structural condition. Treat every flag as a reason to look, not a finding.

Pick an area

Each area is a separate page built from its own photos. Counts below are model estimates, computed from the same data the page shows.

How it works

  1. Find the poles. Mapillary's map features mark where its detector saw a utility pole. For each one, the photos where the pole appears largest are fetched, and the pole is cropped out using Mapillary's own outline.
  2. Look at each photo. A vision model answers a fixed set of questions about every crop: is this a utility pole or a street light, wood or steel, does it lean, is the crossarm intact, is a transformer visible, are branches touching, how many communication attachments. It also says where in the photo it saw each thing.
  3. Combine and show. Photos of the same pole are merged into one record with a vote per field and the disagreement kept as a reliability signal. Where a street was photographed in several years, the photos can be compared side by side. Detected poles are also checked against OpenStreetMap.

What it cannot do

A photo does not show rot, ground-line decay, loading, ownership, or compliance. Old, blurred, or distorted images mislead. In wooded areas a branch behind the pole reads as touching it. A pole with no flag has not been inspected. The useful output is a worklist of things worth a look, with the photo attached, not a condition rating.
