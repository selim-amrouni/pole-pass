Who owns these poles

Poles in Newton are jointly owned: the electric utility and the telecom company that shares the pole each own part of it, and each is responsible for moving its own lines. When a pole is replaced, the old one usually stays up until the last party has transferred its cables. Which party is due to move next — and so when any particular old pole is due to come down — is not published anywhere this page can read. This page therefore assigns no owner, no maintainer, and no responsibility to any record, and a double-pole flag here says nothing about whose work is outstanding.

Why double poles

Double poles in Newton are on the public record in the Massachusetts joint pole-attachment rulemaking DPU 26-10 / DTC 26-1, in comments filed by a Newton resident rather than by the city; the Departments held their public hearing in May 2026. That proceeding is a matter of public record and is not connected to this page, which is an independent read of public street photographs.

What a double-pole flag is

A flag means a vision model, shown one photograph containing two pole detections, read it as an old pole left standing beside its replacement. It is a candidate for someone to look at, not an inspection and not a finding. Nobody has visited any of these poles. The distance between the two poles is measured from their outlines in that photograph, which is an estimate from a single viewpoint, not a survey. A pole carrying no flag has not been cleared — most often it means no recent photograph showed it clearly enough to judge.
